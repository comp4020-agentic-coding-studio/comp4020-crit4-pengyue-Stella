# Crit 4 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was when I stopped using passing tests as the sign that the instrument was finished.

My first planting interaction worked correctly. The tests passed and every click produced a sound. But when I actually played with it, it was boring. Each plant made a short sound and then the sound disappeared. I could plant many things, but they never became music together.

That made me change an important part of my original plan. Instead of letting mature plants fade away, I kept them alive as quiet sustained sounds. Suddenly the garden could build up over time.

Later I used the same kind of listening for watering, the different plant species and the mushroom rhythm.

For this crit, listening was more useful than another automated check. The tests could tell me that sound was being generated. They could not tell me whether I wanted to keep playing.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work made me think more carefully about what I should trust the agent to decide.

Claude Code was useful for building the audio system and implementing changes quickly. However, it could not decide whether the result sounded good or whether an interaction was obvious to a new player. I had to play it myself and reject some technically correct results.

I want to keep working this way. I can use automated checks for things that have a clear correct answer. For experience, interaction and sound, I still need to test the result myself.

I do not want “the code works” to automatically mean “the product is finished.”
